## Video Games

I played more No Man's Sky, I put in more hours into it than most games recently so that is a plus. The story is told in an interesting way and is pretty good overall. Gameplay is simple, but varied and doesn't waste too much time (something that always annoys me about survival games is how they don't respect your time). There is enough choice in the gameplay to allow the player some creative freedom in how they play. I'm playing on normal settings right now. There is grinding, but it isn't too excessive and encourages you to explore more planets and combat can be tough at first, but has yet to be punishing. Maybe I've not played enough yet, but I feel that there could be more enemy varieties and weapon types could mechanically matter more. What is there is good enough to sink countless hours into though. A perfect game to play while having a slow moment at work or when needing some alone/gaming time, but don't have the energy for some complex RPG while still wanting something more mindful than the average mobile game. 

Also played Dread Delusion, but it doesn't feel like the kind of game I can pause and leave in the background and quickly jump into it like NMS so I've played less of it. It is an amazing RPG though that gives off some Morrowind vibes. This game was on my Going to Play soon list for a while, but Wouter [wrote about it](https://jefklakscodex.com/games/pc/dread-delusion/) and that convinced me to pick it up. Really awesome game that I need to sink more time into.

## Links

### Articles

Nic Tringali [made a game and that comes with a lot of creative drudgery](https://blog.enceledean.com/creative-drudgery/). I think this is something we all feel and sometimes there is real temptation to eliminate it by creating new tools or using existing ones. Sometimes that is the right call, but often that is alienating us from our work and harms our ability to learn and understand what it is that we are doing. A great little read. Found this one via [Wouter's own Monthly favorites post](https://brainbaking.com/post/2026/07/june-2026/).

Michał Sapka is [not using RSS anymore for consumption](https://michal.sapka.pl/2026/no-more-rss/). I personally think that RSS is a great thing depending on how you use it, but it is true that the RSS feed eventually gets full of backlogged posts that you will likely never read. RSS readers also often display the articles inside themselves so many people don't actually visit the lovely websites that people have created. RSS isn't working for Michał and that is okay, not ever tool needs to work for every person. Instead, he is now using browser bookmarks and link posts to find and keep up with things. It is a different modality of interacting with the web. Nowadays most of us have information pulled towards us (RSS, social media feeds, etc.), but this can be overwhelming. Michał is moving towards a world where he goes out and seeks the interesting things on the internet instead of pulling them towards him. 

### Videos

Bitluni is working on [a RISK-V super-cluster computer](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qMR3IXF2sWw). This is a seriously impressive project that must have been just as awful as it was fun to design and make. He visualizes the cluster very well by giving each CPU an LED to control so at the very least it is a special for the eyes. The fact that someone with the knowhow has the ability to work on something like this from home just because they can is a testament that not everything is bad and wrong with the world. We can make cluster computers on such scale as this in our homes if we know how, something that would have been impossible just 20 years ago. Bitluni made this awesome project and video, go check it out.

Type Ashton talks about the [problems Europe faces with climate change and why "Just install AC" is not a perfect option](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JDUhbt3z-Hk). I feel terrible for those who are suffering with the heatwaves hitting the European continent, especially those who live in countries where AC was never needed. I lived in a place where temps could easily go past 100° F (~38° C) during the summers all my life and didn't have access to AC for a large part of my childhood. We were blessed with a dry climate that made swamp coolers practical, but we didn't have access to that till I was in high school. I know how heat can be. The worst thing though, this heat that is hitting Europe and to a lesser extent the place I live right now, it is a humid heat. Humidity means swamp coolers don't work, sweat is not effective, and normally tolerable temps become deadly. The world is getting hotter and we need to improve indoor climate infrastructure across the world. I feel a strong anger at the current US administration for denying climate change and rolling back efforts to fight it, what happens when the world is pushed too far by the greed of the few in power?  

Soiboi soft builds pneumatic soft robotics. This time around he built [a really cool segmented display that stores its state](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E1BLGpE5zH0). Technically it uses electricity for the air compressor and the microcontroller, but the display and the physical logic is done with air and clever engineering. Really cool stuff.

J-Rat Techworks built [a LoRa gateway to allow sensors to report to Home Assistant over LoRa](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x1i25vBsSUU). LoRa is awesome technology that allows for low energy, long range wireless communication. It is decentralized and encrypted, making it a very useful and safe option for all sorts of projects. If you have a large property you may want to take a look into it as using WiFi or even Zigbee and/or Z-Wave will only get you so far. I live in an apartment though so this project is more theoretically cool than useful to me. Maybe one day. 

Han showcases some [3D prints he designed to improve the shortcomings of his camera gear](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vR1hF_N6i_c). I think this is awesome and really shows how 3D printing gives people the freedom to achieve ownership over the things they buy. Nothing is perfect, but with a 3D printer and a little time you can create almost any solution you need to make things slightly closer to it.
