## Video Games

My partner got me a Switch 2 for my birthday. I would not have gotten myself one and even said "I might get one if my Switch 1 breaks, but not till then". Well, she got me one so I had to play it. Due to the impossibly high prices of everything these days, Nintendo included a choice of one of three games to download with the purchase price of the console. I chose Pokémon Pokopia and it has been a lot of fun.

Pokémon Pokopia (Switch 2) was worked on by the same team as Dragon Quest Builders and shows some of that design principle. You build habitats for Pokémon as you try to discover the mystery of what happened to the humans in the world. You find out rather quickly that it was climate change/natural disasters and humans went somewhere else (space is what the lore sprinkled through the game is pointing at, but I've not completed the game yet). I put in over 20 hours into the game (a lot for me who doesn't really complete games) and can safely say it is fun. Also it fixed the awful camera that prevented me from enjoying Dragon Quest Builders 2 so that is a plus. I'm getting close to the end of the main game, but took a break to play the only other game I've played on the Switch 2 so far.

Atelier Resleriana: The Red Alchemist & the White Guardian (Switch) is the latest mainline Atelier game released. I'm still only at the start of the second chapter in the game, but so far the characters have been charming and the gameplay a lot more fun than the game that came before it (Yumia was so poorly optimized I dropped it). The Switch 2's ability to play Switch 1 games in full resolution in handheld mode and the significantly faster loading times made this really pleasant to play. I'll probably stick to it for a while before bouncing back to Pokopia to complete the main story in that. I really like Atelier games and hope that Resleriana continues to be fun. 

I'm not likely to buy many Switch 2 games for a while as they are so expensive, but all my Switch 1 games should work and they should work better so I'm a lot more motivated to play them now. Graphics are not too important, but stable frame rates and quicker load times are wonderful improvements. If Nintendo does go digital only though, I'm going to just buy games from GOG for my much more powerful PC.

## Movies

My partner is a huge fan of the old Masters of the Universe TV series so naturally we ended up watching the abomination that is the Amazon Masters of the Universe (2026) movie. I've not seen a movie that hates its source material and own message as much as this one. Every aspect of the franchise is reduced to a joke about how funny and childish it is. Don't get me wrong, the TV series was objectively not great and mostly a tool for selling toys, but it wasn't ashamed to be a cartoon for kids. The only saving grace was Skeletor, and even then, he was only given a few great lines in an otherwise bad movie. 

I do not recommend it, but my partner wants to subject a guest we are having over this week to it so I'm going to be seeing it again. Also there was some quarter-baked message about masculinity that got the same treatment the rest of this did, there is no message, but it borderline acts like it is about to say something important and it is just barely self-aware enough to feel ashamed about it. They should have had someone proofread the script and whoever was in charge should likely never be allowed to touch the movie industry again. My partner agrees that it is awful, but that just makes her want to subject more people to it.

### Articles

Antonio Santos reminds us that while RSS is awesome, we should [still visit the personal blogs of people every once in a while](https://antoniosantos.io/your-rss-reader-is-robbing-you/) to see the unique design and hard work the creators of said blogs put into them. I generally use Newsboat to collect feeds and then open them up in my web browser, MPV, or whatever program best suits that particular feed for this very reason. There are some websites out there with pretty bad design that benefit from being formatted in a way I control, but most of the time small websites have cool designs worth seeing. 

Matthew Cambion [talks about his idea of America](https://starbreaker.org/opinions/an-idea-of-america.html). A very anarchist take that I can mostly get behind. Being American is not about being born in the U.S. or having political loyalty to the country, it is about personal sovereignty and working towards improving the ability of all to exercise that personal sovereignty. At least, that was how I interpreted Matthew's article. There is a lot more in there and a lot of good insight into a more anarchist interpretation of the American "identity" in face of the current regime's rhetoric.

Andros Fenollosa talks about [IndieWeb technology and philosophy](https://en.andros.dev/blog/0b8e451e/i-joined-the-indieweb-heres-what-i-learned/). It is interesting to see how other people interact and see the IndieWeb group and their leverage of technology to create interaction between the independent web and the siloed off corporate web. There is a lot of valid criticism about the technology and experiments the IndieWeb has recommended, but they do server a purpose and are at least fun to tinker with. Hope that it works out for whatever Andros's goal is. Anyway, if you want a digestible summary of the IndieWeb's wiki, this is a good place to start. Andros has a cool project by the way called [Org Social](https://org-social.org). It is a social network built around org mode. Not something I'd use, but I thought it would be cool to share.

Wouter Groeneveld wrote [a guide to writing personal letters](https://brainbaking.com/post/2026/08/a-beginners-guide-to-writing-snail-mail/). To be honest, this guide is more of a fun read than a guide on formal writing and that is probably for the best. Letters are more fun when they are fun and we already have too much seriousness in our lives. Anyway, I just thought it was funny that Wouter posted this around the same time a friend of mine I've not talked to in a while texted me "want to start writing letters to each other again?". I'm going to be sending some snail mail myself soon and it is really something people need to do more often. My handwriting is terrible and I'm bad at reading American standard cursive (I can, but just not fast), so I get discouraged easily, but you can't get better without practice and writing to a friend is a great way to get that practice.

### Videos

Gabriela de Mendonça Gomes talks about [the erotic, how it differs from the pornographic, and how eroticism is important](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gd2ys1V3fm4). She does explore this concept through literature, but her idea applies globally. Eroticism's goal is the celebration and creation of desire, whereas pornography's goal is to end desire as if it were some task that needs to be completed. I have a lot of thoughts on this subject, so many I've rewritten my little comment here many times. Sounds like I need to write an article about it. In any case, I do agree with her premise, but I do also think there are underlying issues that have lead to the proliferation of the pornographic over the erotic: they spawn from the puritan culture that still flows through the U.S. and the way porn is easily commodifiable under capitalism. I also don't think the pornographic is necessarily an evil that must be shunned altogether, but it is something that is problematic in a society such as ours where Eros is shunned.

Eva Deverell from Lady Writer shares a rather cute and fun way of [generating subjects to study and explore in depth](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9657e55Gsc0) as well as a method for studying them. She basically gives you a tabletop RPG style generator for picking your next PHD subject. I've got lots of interests that I've been neglecting and wanting to get into. Maybe stealing her exploratory framework would be a fun way to pick something out. Anyway, it is a fun video about learning and learning should be fun. 

Erick (with a K) hosted a secret santa style [gift exchange between a bunch of YouTube engineers and machinists called Tool Room Takeover](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLX3aHl-pXyv8). Basically a bunch of cool people making cool gifts for each other. Everything from clocks and fountain pens to a fully forged filing vice and a musical instrument. The link is to a playlist of all the videos. 

Justin Davies [made a pizza from a pear tree](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zNwLgWhVHCw). I've shared before his tree ice creams, now he has done a pizza. 

Full Stack Woodworking made a [sculpture that shows the passage of time](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D6EAjrEmDDo). It is an interesting project and even integrates with home assistant. 

Alien Bob talks about [the game and media series called A Force More Powerful](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dmL7Ph4Us8o). These game was supposedly designed to help promote and train nonviolent resistance movements. It also is very likely US propaganda designed to undermine real resistance movements and destabilize the United States' ideological enemies. He covers the history of A Force More Powerful, talks about how real resistance movements start with people and not abstract number sheets (unless you are a CIA agent), and then mentions the many evils going on in society that we should organize against. Very interesting to know that BreakAway Games basically makes pro-capitalist propaganda and milliary training tools. 

Campbell Walker from Struthless talks about [the DMCA and how copyright protections made a great tool for the techno dystopia we have today](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aLPrNSFLb2U). He uses this as a great example of how dystopias form by making us accept small compromises that seem too boring to care about in the moment, but add up. There is a lot of good stuff here and is worth watching. 

Dinki gives [their backstory and a bit of philosophy on life](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sQu6CFAojSU). Dinki makes some awesome art projects out of Linux and has a unique style. This has garnered some questions from people and they are happy to answer. Dinki goes over depression, experimentation with psychedelics, life being neurodivergent, and a lot of other things. It is a very interesting video and I'd recommend giving it time, especially if you enjoy looking at or using their custom Linux desktop rices.
